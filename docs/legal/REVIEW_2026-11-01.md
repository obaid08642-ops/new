### What the payouts code does (checked, files named)
- A completed service creates a `provider_earning` ledger entry in state `pending`; `LedgerService.matureEscrow` makes it `cleared` once `available_at` passes. The delay is `finance_config.settlement.delay_days[type]` or the default, 3 days (`SETTLEMENT_DELAY_DAYS`) (`finance-engine.module.ts`, `commission-defaults.ts`).
- The **provider** requests a withdrawal (`provider-payouts.controller.ts`): minimum `payout_schedule.minimum_payout_sar`, default 100; the IBAN must be verified and match `SA` plus 22 digits; the amount is locked; a second request is refused while one is open.
- The admin approves (`finance-suite.service.ts`): `PENDING_ADMIN_APPROVAL` -> `APPROVED_FOR_PAYOUT` -> paid; at or above a threshold a second, different admin must approve (`AWAITING_SECOND_APPROVAL`).
- The balance may go **negative** (ledger comment "available may go NEGATIVE"), which is what clause 5.9 describes.
- `finance_config.payout_schedule` = weekly/Sunday/3 processing days exists as configuration, but **no code reads `frequency` or `day`**: nothing runs a weekly payout. The contract therefore does not promise one. After approval the transfer time is "the normal bank-transfer cycle": the code has no number.

### Gaps the lead should know (no code changed)
1. **"First N orders" promotions.** `CommissionResolver` and `setRule` support a rate with `effective_from`/`effective_to` (date window) and `percent` 0 to 100. There is **no order counter**. The owner asked for "a period or first N orders", so 5.1 says so, but the admin can only enforce the period today. Either add a counter or limit the wording to a period.
2. **Delivery fees collected by the platform.** The VAT sentence follows the owner. The code has `delivery_fee` on the provider profile (the pharmacy's own fee) and a refund rule for the delivery fee; I found **no place where the platform collects a delivery fee for itself**. The clause is conditional ("where it provides the delivery and states the fee before the order"), so it is harmless if there is none.
3. **The invoice code still follows Model B (#2362).** The contract now commits to Model A. Until `seller()`, `EInvoice` and `invoice-pdf.ts` are changed as listed in section 5 ("What code must change, Model A"), invoices contradict clause 5.6.
4. The other legal texts (`01_PATIENT_TERMS`, `02_PRIVACY_POLICY`, `04_TELEHEALTH_CONSENT`) still contain `[ ]` blanks (for example the trade name and the city). They were not part of this task.

### Competitor terms: what was really reached
Only **Tamara's merchant terms** (a true merchant agreement) and **Vezeeta's generic site terms** were opened. Tabby (navigation shell only), Cura (404), HungerStation (404), Jahez (403) and Careem (404) were not retrievable, and Seha Virtual, Nahdi, Mrsool and Noon were not tried. Altibbi's doctor agreement was read in the first pass. So "adopt the standard clauses" rests on three documents, not on the whole market. Clause ideas taken, in our own wording: reserves and holds with a stated reason; repayment of a shortfall within 7 days; a fee-change notice with a right to object and leave (Tamara gives 10 days; we give 30); mutual confidentiality with a tail (Tamara 3 years; same); weaker party's assignment needs consent, not unreasonably withheld; a liability cap tied to fees with indirect loss excluded; Arabic prevails; KYC/AML. **Deliberately not adopted:** acceptance by silence or by posting, changes that apply at once, "any other reason it deems necessary" as a termination ground, foreign law and courts (Egypt, UK), and Tamara's 60-day termination notice.

### 2.1 Note on 5.1: percentage commission and the Anti-Bribery Law

One secondary source (Baker McKenzie summary of the Health Professions Law) says that requesting or accepting commissions or benefits for directing patients is prohibited conduct. The lead's list asks for "no referral fees/kickbacks". The platform's commission is a percentage of the price of a booking the platform processes. **The lawyer should confirm that this percentage is acceptable as a platform service fee** (platform services: listing, booking, payment, support) and not as a referral commission. Possible fallbacks, only if the lawyer asks: a fixed per-booking fee, or a subscription. Nothing is changed in the code for this.

## 5. Decision memo: who issues the invoice to the patient

### The conflict

- **v1.0, 5.4:** the provider issues the service invoice in its own name; the platform invoices only its commission.
- **The product (#2362, `billing/invoice-pdf.ts` and `billing.module.ts`):** the **platform is the issuer** ("الجهة المصدرة للفاتورة", the seller is `finance_config` `invoice` or `ZATCA_SELLER_NAME`/`ZATCA_VAT_NUMBER`). The provider appears only as an information block with the note "the issuer of this invoice is the company above". VAT is computed **inside the whole total at 15%** (`subtotal = total - vat`).
- **The settlement config says the opposite on VAT:** `DEFAULT_COMMISSIONS.tax` = "KSA VAT applied on commission only".

So the platform already invoices the patient for the whole amount with VAT on the whole amount, while its own finance config assumes VAT only on its commission. This is the G7 problem the lead named.

### Model A: agent / marketplace (the provider is the supplier)

- The service invoice names the **provider as seller** (its VAT number in the QR). The platform may generate and send it as a **third-party billing** agent. The platform issues a second invoice to the provider for the **commission**, with 15% VAT on the commission only.
- **VAT:** the platform's taxable supply is its commission. The provider's VAT on the service depends on its own registration and on how the service is rated (see section 4: sources conflict).
- **ZATCA:** third-party billing needs a written agreement between supplier and issuer, is subject to ZATCA approval (the agreement is submitted), the invoice carries a third-party flag and a statement that it is issued on the seller's behalf, only one invoice per supply, and the seller remains liable for its content (secondary sources **[U]**).
- **SAMA:** the platform still collects the patient's payment and settles to providers. A licensed payment provider must hold and settle the funds, or the platform must be one **[U]**. This applies to both models.
- **Fits:** v1.0 text (1.2 "technical intermediary", 3.2 provider's full responsibility), the Health Professions and SFDA rules (the platform neither practises medicine nor sells medicines), and the platform's disclaimer of medical liability.
- **Costs:** providers must be on e-invoicing; non-VAT-registered providers need a no-VAT invoice; the third-party agreement must be filed per provider.

### Model B: principal / reseller (the platform is the supplier to the patient)

- The platform invoices the patient (as built in #2362) and separately **buys** the service from the provider (the provider invoices the platform, or the platform self-bills).
- **VAT:** the platform's supply is the **whole price**, 15% on the gross; the provider's supply to the platform is a second layer. If healthcare is exempt or zero-rated for the provider, the two layers do not match.
- **Conflicts:** a non-licensed company selling health services or medicines (SFDA: retail only through retail pharmacies; Health Professions rules); the platform becomes the seller in consumer-law terms (refunds, warranty, E-Commerce Law duties) and in the eyes of the patient the party responsible for the medical service, which contradicts Article 1.2 and 3.2.

### Recommendation: Model A

Model A matches the contract, the licensing rules and the finance config, and limits the platform's VAT base to its commission. Model B is only appropriate if the owner wants the platform to be the seller and is ready to be licensed and to carry that liability.

### What code must change, per model (not done here)

**Model A:**
1. `billing.module.ts` `seller()`: for service invoices read the **provider's** legal name, VAT number, CR and address (`provider.tax_number`, `vat_number`; the profile already has `tax_number`), and keep the platform values for the commission invoice.
2. `EInvoice` model and `invoice-pdf.ts`: the issuer block becomes the provider, with a "third-party issued on behalf of" statement and the flag; the provider block's note ("issuer is the company above") must change.
3. A second document type for the **commission invoice** to the provider (VAT on commission only), generated from the `commission-ledger`.
4. The QR (`tlvQr`) uses the provider's name and VAT number; providers without a VAT number need a non-tax receipt path.
5. ZATCA onboarding: the third-party billing agreement (this contract, Article 5.5) filed per provider, plus the onboarding of the platform's solution for each seller (secondary sources) **[U]**.
6. The "VAT on the whole total" calculation applies only if the provider is VAT-registered and the service is rated; read from the provider.

**Model B:**
1. `billing` stays as built; `§5.5` and `§1.2` of the contract change to a sale/purchase structure.
2. The finance engine: VAT on the gross (`finance_config.tax` note and the ledger), a purchase invoice (or self-billing) from each provider, and input-VAT handling.
3. Licensing review before launch (health services and medicines); consumer-law terms (refund, warranty) move to the platform.
