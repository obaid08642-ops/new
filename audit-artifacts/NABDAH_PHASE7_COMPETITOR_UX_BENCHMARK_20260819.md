# Phase 7 — Competitor research and screen-by-screen UX benchmark

## Scope, method and limitation

This benchmark compares Nabdah’s **source-audited journeys** with publicly published product descriptions, app-store listings and official service pages for regional and global reference products. It is not a claim that an external product’s unauthenticated public site proves its entire clinical, financial or provider backend. Authenticated flows, insurance adjudication and provider operations were not impersonated, and no account was created on a competitor. Competitor patterns are therefore **design inputs**, not a justification to relax Nabdah’s security, consent, ownership or clinical-safety requirements.

The review covers the user-requested categories: consultations, pharmacy, laboratory/diagnostics, radiology/records, home care, mental health, nutrition, maternity, and medication adherence. It also captures the limited but relevant provider-side public descriptions available for Vezeeta and Labayh.

## Research findings at a glance

| Category | Observable benchmark pattern | Nabdah implication |
|---|---|---|
| Consultation | One clear service choice, patient-preference filters, real availability, a prepared visit, then a durable care plan | Make clinic, video and home consultation separate, explicit service contracts with truthful slot, price and payment/insurance state; remove all auto-confirmation/fabricated details. |
| Pharmacy | Prescription upload/validation, delivery **or** pickup, substitutions/fulfilment, tracking, and pharmacist help | Complete a canonical order lifecycle and show it as a timeline; do not decrement inventory or claim delivery before the assigned pharmacy confirms it. |
| Diagnostics | A booking can be confirmed, rescheduled or cancelled; a result is a private longitudinal record, not a generic link | Unify lab/radiology booking and result ownership; make collection/exam/result/report states visible and authenticated. |
| Home care | Select service, time, location and patient/family profile, then receive confirmation, reminders and live status | Use location only after purpose consent, make caregiver assignment explicit and support only lawful tracking while on an active visit. |
| Mental health | A privacy-led entry, immediate or scheduled session choice, verified specialist fit, modality choice and crisis exit | Do not let mood logging or an AI response masquerade as care; use clear safety escalation and protected session state. |
| Nutrition and maternity | Goal-based onboarding, source-labelled self-tracking, progressive care plan and calendar/reminders | Treat all inputs as patient-entered/self-reported until clinically verified; never create pregnancy, diet or medical state from fallbacks. |
| Medication adherence | Practical dose schedule, taken/skipped logging, caregiver permission and progress report | Fix interval mapping/precision, bind notification schedules to a canonical prescription/reminder record and require consent for any family alert. |

## Patient experience comparison — discovery, booking and care

| Nabdah screen / decision point | Source-audit gap recorded in Phases 2–6 | Public benchmark pattern | Phase 8 design and contract requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home and service discovery | Service entry points exist but cross-service visual vocabulary, translation coverage and RTL/LTR behavior are inconsistent; bottom navigation forces RTL for every language. | Vezeeta places clinic, telehealth, home visit, pharmacy and diagnostic entry points in one explicit service choice surface; it begins discovery with specialty, location and search. [1] | Establish six-locale direction-aware tokens; one semantic icon set; a service launcher with title, eligibility, expected next step and an honest availability indicator. The locale decides direction, not a global forced RTL flag. |
| Doctor/facility search | Current public-care details can expose inactive/unpublished providers and broad documents. | Vezeeta provides specialty, appointment type, title, gender, availability, fee and payment filters, then sort; it distinguishes phone and video. [2] | Use only allowlisted active/published profiles. Give clinic, video and home a service-type pill; disclose qualification, language, verified review policy, fee basis, insurance eligibility and **real** next slot. |
| Doctor profile | Public DTO is not allowlisted and similar-provider query can include inactive records. | Vezeeta presents service-relevant specialty, fee and verified-visit review context; Cura explains modalities, response expectation and follow-up rule. [1] [3] | Build a deliberate public profile DTO: verified credentials, specialties, services, fee/coverage disclosure, languages, cancellation rule, accessible slots and only consented clinic media. Do not expose raw profile, KYC, exact private address or internal schedules. |
| Consultation choice | Clinic/video/home, cash/insurance and asynchronous paths are not consistently distinguished; insurance flow can auto-confirm. | Teladoc’s public journey uses a brief intake, provider choice, phone/video visit and an accessible follow-up care plan. Cura separates immediate, specialist and program services with stated response/service expectations. [4] [3] | Create a single `ConsultationIntent` review screen: modality, patient, provider/facility, slot or response SLA, price components, coverage decision state, payment state, cancellation deadline and clinical suitability. Insurance is always `pending` until backend decision. |
| Slot selection and confirmation | Monthly report uses wrong slot field and state transitions may bypass workflow engine. | Vezeeta lets patients choose an available day/time; its public copy states provider-defined working hours and booking notification. [1] [2] | Slot cards must be server-generated with timezone, capacity and state version. Confirmation must be idempotent and create a canonical booking; show an immutable receipt with join/arrival guidance. |
| Pre-visit intake | AI and medical-input features include mismatched contracts/local fallbacks. | Teladoc asks questions before the visit so the clinician is prepared and supports pre-visit document/image upload. [4] | Capture reason, symptom severity, consent, attachments and red flags as server-backed draft. Add emergency routing, data purpose and expiry; never use AI output as a diagnosis or substitute for clinical review. |
| Visit room / chat / call | Realtime generic room join and waiting-room authorization are unsafe; family chat uses legacy membership data. | Cura describes text, voice-note, voice and video in-app contact; Teladoc describes phone/video visit and an ongoing care team. [3] [4] | Enter only from a canonical appointment participant record. Provide waiting, connection, retry and reconnect states; close the room per policy; retain/document messages under least privilege; fail closed on membership uncertainty. |
| Care plan, prescription and follow-up | Local/fabricated doctor EHR/prescription flows exist in Provider; medical orders are not consistently canonical. | Teladoc exposes post-visit plan, prescriptions, labs and next steps in the patient account; Cura describes a defined 14-day review window. [4] [3] | A signed care-plan screen must distinguish clinician-issued prescription, lab/radiology order, referral and self-care advice, each with issuer/time/version/status. Provide follow-up only when policy and appointment state allow it. |
| Cash, insurance and payment | Insurance approval-to-payment and split-payment routes have critical contract gaps; payment retry/webhook/transaction controls are unsafe. | Cura separates insurance-supported specialist advice from non-covered immediate/program services and asks for insurance/payment selection rather than assuming approval. [3] | The total screen must show service fee, verified coverage decision, patient liability, payment method, intent status and gateway error. No “paid”, basket fulfilment, provider payout or receipt until signed verified payment event and idempotent ledger state. |
| Pharmacy prescription entry | Diagnostics booking and several commerce paths display fabricated fee/tax/appointment data; chronic refill mutates inventory too early. | Nahdi publishes prescription upload, delivery/pickup and pharmacist support; Al-Dawaa publishes delivery/pickup, secure saved payment, barcode scan, image request and order tracking. [5] [6] | Use a clinical prescription validation/review step, clear OTC vs prescription distinction, an allowed substitute decision by the patient, a price/availability quote with expiry and a pharmacy-confirmed fulfilment lifecycle. |
| Pharmacy cart and fulfilment | Legacy/parallel pharmacy collections, wrong endpoint calls, early stock mutation and bad reorder/refill parameters create divergent truth. | Pharmacy references make pickup, delivery, payment and tracking separate user choices. [5] [6] | One canonical order ID across patient/provider/admin. Timeline: `draft → quote_pending → pharmacy_assigned → pharmacist_review → patient_approval → payment_pending/verified → preparing → dispatched/ready_for_pickup → completed`, with guarded cancellation/shortage/substitution branches. Inventory reserve/commit must be atomic and auditable. |
| Lab appointment and home collection | Booking UI has fake financial/appointment facts and provider operations bypass workflow state transitions. | Al Borg’s published listing includes booking, rescheduling, cancellation and report viewing. Saudi public home healthcare shows appointment detail notification and the appointment list as the patient-facing record. [7] [8] | Build a single diagnostic booking receipt with collection type, facility/location, appointment, preparation instructions, insurance/cash state and status timeline. Every reschedule/cancel/reassign changes the canonical lifecycle and notifies both sides. |
| Radiology booking/report | Report upload uses fabricated links and foreign/cross-scope report access needs hardening. | SEHA publishes patient access to medical records, lab and radiology results, scheduling and authorized family access. [9] | Separate imaging order, booked exam, performed exam, radiologist report and media retrieval. Use private signed access only for appointment participants/authorized family; never show a fabricated report URL or a report before clinical release. |
| Results and longitudinal record | Monthly health report references `scheduled_at` instead of canonical slot field; privacy/ownership has unresolved gaps. | SEHA centralizes records, lab/radiology results, measurements, allergies and family access; EHS describes secure integrated clinical records and report requests. [9] [10] | Create a timeline organized by source and release state: order, collection/exam, result, clinician review, report, recommended follow-up. Add clear origin labels, access log and dedicated, revocable family-share controls. |
| Home nursing/care request | Patient and Provider routes/responses are incomplete or fabricated; location/emergency consent remains fail-closed. | Call Doctor describes a sequence of service → date/time/location → patient profile → confirm/pay → team arrival, with status updates; Saudi government home-care guidance illustrates referral eligibility and appointment notification rather than a generic unqualified request. [11] [8] | Add service eligibility/triage, patient and address confirmation, lawful location consent, transparent service/visit fee, assigned team identity, arrival window and time-bounded visit tracking. Do not activate SOS/QR/location operations before legal/product approval. |
| Mental-health landing / matching | Mood journal submits fixed values and wrong fields; local clinical fallbacks and AI response contracts are unsafe. | Labayh presents immediate and scheduled sessions, specialty/budget filtering, modality choice, programs, assessments and a mood tracker. BetterHelp uses needs/preferences matching, choice of communication mode and a specific crisis exclusion. [12] [13] | Use voluntary, editable mood inputs and a safe screening exit. Offer immediate vs scheduled care only if real provider capacity exists. Explain privacy, clinician credentials, price/coverage and explicitly route emergency/crisis users away from unsupported flows. |
| Nutrition plan | AI plan response contract mismatch and broken save action mean users can see advice that is not persistently actionable. | Noom combines user logging, goals, progressive education, tracking, optional coaching and device connections; it labels nutrition as behavior support rather than silent clinical prescription. [14] | Save only validated, versioned plan objects from backend; show data source, author/AI disclosure, clinical-review status, allergies/contraindications flags and patient acknowledgment. Offer a revision path and never invent calories or meal plan contents. |
| Maternity/cycle/pregnancy | Clinical state can fall back to locally stored pregnancy data. | Ovia begins with a user-selected journey and supports dates, symptoms, milestones, postpartum mode, controlled sharing and PIN protection. [15] | Explicitly choose cycle, trying-to-conceive, pregnancy, postpartum or menopause. Persist user-entered source/date/consent; support loss/termination/opt-out as respectful first-class states; do not infer medical pregnancy status. |
| Medication reminder and refill | Monthly cadence maps incorrectly to weekly, dose precision is lost, refill lacks order ID, and reminder state is not robustly server-bound. | Medisafe combines schedule/reminders, dose history, measurements, interaction alerts, progress reports and opt-in caregiver support. [16] | Store exact dose, route, frequency, timezone and recurrence on a canonical medication record; a reminder action writes taken/skipped/snoozed event with idempotency. Family alert is opt-in; drug interaction content requires approved source/legal review. |
| Profile, family, privacy and support | Family permissions mismatch; privacy export/deletion and consent screens are not enforced. | SEHA exposes authorized family access as an explicit request; Ovia exposes controlled sharing and account PIN; competitor descriptions repeatedly make privacy explicit. [9] [15] | Build a single relationship/consent center showing invite, pending, role, resource scope, expiry/revocation and access log. Export/delete/consent controls remain unavailable until an enforceable backend/legal workflow exists—never show a deceptive successful state. |

## Provider and operational experience comparison

| Provider workflow screen | Benchmark evidence | Nabdah requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Onboarding and credential approval | Labayh’s consultant listing describes certificate/license upload, interview, activation and training before patient requests are available. [17] | Keep provider in a visibly `pending_review` state with document completeness, status reason, next action and no access to live requests or payouts until approval. |
| Inbox / service queue | Labayh describes checking patient requests before chat/call; Vezeeta describes appointment schedule and patient database management. [17] [18] | Each service type needs its own authoritative queue, filtered by facility/branch/assignment and SLA. No local fabricated queue or free-text direct assignment. Accept/reject/escalate transitions must use the workflow engine and notify the patient. |
| Calendar, shifts and multiple sites | Vezeeta describes edit/adjust appointment and multiple-clinic management. [18] | Canonical availability must support clinic/facility, service mode, roster, leave, slot capacity and blackout. Patient slots must be generated from the same model; edits need conflict detection and patient reschedule workflow. |
| Visit and clinical documentation | Labayh consultant describes messaging/calls and a recent public release references prescriptions/medical reports and referral reason. [17] | Use appointment-bound workspace with intake, consent, notes, orders, prescription/report release, discharge/no-show and follow-up; all clinical data must be source-backed, signed and access-scoped. |
| Pharmacy/lab/radiology/nursing fulfilment | Pharmacy/logistics and diagnostics experiences make tracking and report completion explicit; home care makes assignment/arrival visible. [5] [6] [7] [11] | Replace broadcast/wrong endpoints and fabricated reports with service-specific fulfilment board, required evidence checklist, accepted substitution/collection/performed/result/release states and exceptions. |
| Payout, wallet and support | Vezeeta’s provider listing references invoices/payments; it also illustrates no-show reduction using confirmation/online payment. [18] | Present ledger entries as pending/available/paid/reversed with source order and immutable reconciliation reference. No optimistic success, local-only transfer, or payout without maker-checker/atomic backend control. |
| Device/security and privacy | The health-category benchmark consistently exposes sensitive data with explicit permission/security statements, but public claims are not proof of technical sufficiency. [9] [10] [17] | Implement actual server-backed session/device/revocation/2FA and role scope before showing “secured” state. Record sensitive view/export and use minimum-data provider DTOs. |

## Premium UI system and navigation direction

The research supports a Nabdah-specific design system rather than a visual copy of any competitor. Phase 8 must build the following foundation before page-by-page styling work.

| System element | Required implementation direction |
|---|---|
| Navigation | A locale-aware bottom navigation for patient high-frequency destinations, a contextual back/close route from all details, and a provider-side queue-first navigation. Avoid more than five primary items; use a labeled overflow/service hub for lower-frequency tools. |
| Information hierarchy | Every service task begins with user goal and care mode, then establishes verified eligibility/availability/price, then asks for irreversible confirmation. Keep a persistent appointment/order status chip and one clear next action. |
| Visual language | Replace emoji and inconsistent glyphs with one licensed/vector icon family. Define semantic tokens for surface, primary, success, warning, critical and clinical-info—not arbitrary per-screen colors. Use calm contrast-safe neutrals, high-emphasis actions and clear destructive-action affordances. |
| Trust cues | Use source-labelled medical data, clinician/facility verification, explicit insurance decision and payment state, revision time and privacy purpose. Never use mock rating, mock price, mock ETA, mock prescription, placeholder report or fabricated “AI diagnosis.” |
| Accessibility | Require scalable text, dynamic content wrapping, semantic labels, keyboard/screen-reader roles where applicable, contrast checks and target-size review. RTL versus LTR applies per selected locale and must be verified on each supported language. |
| Localization | All six locales need one translation key per visible text, plural and date/number/medical-unit formatting rules, locale-correct direction and zero raw key/fallback English/Arabic fragments. Translation completeness is a build gate, not a manual visual hope. |

## Prioritized Phase 8 UX implementation backlog

| Priority | Delivery package | Acceptance boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **P0** | Trust and clinical/financial truthfulness | Remove fabricated consultation/diagnostic/prescription/report/payment data; split insurance decision from payment; enforce canonical workflow states and all ownership gates. |
| **P0** | Security-led care room and public discovery | Allowlisted public provider DTO, active/published filters, assigned-participant realtime room, revocable family access and signed private media/report delivery. |
| **P0** | Locale/RTL foundation | Locale-driven direction; shared design tokens; premium vector icons; no hardcoded source strings or forced direction; six-locale coverage test. |
| **P1** | Consultation and diagnostics journeys | Intent/slot/receipt/status/care-plan screens, service-specific cancellation/reschedule and consistent order/booking timeline. |
| **P1** | Pharmacy and medication safety | One order model and timeline, deferred atomic inventory, safe substitutions, exact dose/cadence and opt-in adherence support. |
| **P1** | Provider service operations | Verified onboarding, authoritative queues, calendar/availability, clinical completion/release and atomic wallet/payout representation. |
| **P1** | Longitudinal health, maternity, nutrition and mental health | User-entered source-backed state; no fallback clinical fact; safe AI disclosure/guardrails; program and mood tracking only when genuinely persisted. |
| **P2** | Progressive premium polish | Motion/accessibility refinements, empty/error/retry states, educational content navigation and future device integrations only after the underlying contract is real. |

## Phase 7 conclusion

The competitive advantage for Nabdah is not adding more unverified surfaces. It is delivering a coherent, multilingual, **truthful and safe** cross-service journey: one user-selected intent, one canonical workflow, transparent price/coverage status, protected care communication, traceable fulfilment and a meaningful next clinical action. The benchmark reinforces, rather than changes, the Phase 2–6 findings: Phase 8 must repair the source contract first and then apply the premium UI system on top of verified data.
