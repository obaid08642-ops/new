# Make the country a config row, not a fork

Nabd Plus should become multi-country the way Amazon, Uber, Delivery Hero and Shopify did: **one codebase and one logical schema, with an ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 market key (`SA`, `AE`, `BH`...) on every market-scoped record, a per-country configuration document, per-country strategy plugins for the concerns that really differ (payments, tax and e-invoicing, licence verification, SMS/OTP, identity, address, legal texts), and data placed in one "cell" per data-residency jurisdiction** rather than one fork per country. Health is the dimension that makes this harder than e-commerce. In every target market a doctor's licence stops at the border (and, in the UAE, at the emirate line). The UAE keeps health data in-country by default. Egypt needs a regulator licence for any foreign hosting. Saudi Arabia has a draft rule that would require in-Kingdom storage. So cross-border consultations should be denied by default, and a second country will usually mean a second hosting cell. The repository's own inventory (`docs/platform/MULTI-COUNTRY.md`, PR #2696) measures the coupling: 81 `'SAR'` literals in 36 files, two VAT sources, one payment provider per deployment, `Asia/Riyadh` in 7 files, Saudi phone rules in 15 files and SCFHS in 24 files. That is a few weeks of mechanical work behind a `countries` collection, and none of it changes behaviour for Saudi users if the order is right: finish R11 P3 (integer halalas) first, add `currency` second. For the second country, the **UAE, starting with Dubai only, is the best commercial fit**. It uses the same PSPs, BNPL and two-decimal currency, and its expatriate population speaks the languages Nabd already ships (a demographic inference, not a sourced figure). It is also the most expensive legally, because it needs an in-UAE cell and DHA licensing. **Bahrain is the cheaper technical alternative**: one regulator, a three-decimal currency that tests the money model, and no confirmed health-hosting mandate. Egypt should come later. Every regulatory item below needs counsel or an accountant to confirm it before code depends on it.

## Large marketplaces share one pattern: one platform, a country key, regional cells

The public engineering evidence converges. **Amazon models each country store as a separate "marketplace" with its own ID, and many seller API operations must say which marketplace they target** (Saudi Arabia is `A17E79C6D8DWNP`, the UAE `A2VIGQ35RCS4UG`) ([Amazon SP-API](https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api/docs/marketplace-ids)). **Uber mapped cities onto "pods" inside regions to shrink the blast radius of failures** ([Uber](https://www.uber.com/en-IN/blog/fulfillment-platform-rearchitecture/)), and it removed zone-specific configuration so that services could move ([Uber](https://www.uber.com/en-gb/blog/up-portable-microservices-ready-for-the-cloud/)). **Delivery Hero, Talabat's owner, described moving from regional platforms to "country-isolated systems" while keeping "a single view of our entire system"** ([New Relic case study, secondary, older](https://newrelic.com/blog/infrastructure-monitoring/how-delivery-hero-scales-for-rapid-growth)). It also folded local platforms such as Turkey's into one global platform ([Delivery Hero](https://www.deliveryhero.com/?p=10554)). AWS guidance treats "supercells" in separate regions as the tool for both disaster recovery and data residency ([AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/solutions/cell-based-architecture-for-amazon-eks/)). A common companion pattern is a small global directory that records each user's home region and routes them there at sign-in ([AWS Builder Center, community post](https://builder.aws.com/content/2dhVhtsciD5gVBlCKUlHoszrDzU/scale-across-borders-build-a-multi-region-architecture-while-maintaining-data-residency)). No first-party architecture material was found for noon, Careem, Talabat or Vezeeta, so their internal designs remain unknown.

Two details settle the choice of key. **Shopify warns that its own market IDs are not stable targeting identifiers and tells apps to target by country** ([Shopify.dev](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/markets)). **MongoDB's zone-sharding tutorial uses a compound shard key `{ country: 1, userid: 1 }` with ISO alpha-2 codes**: documents in a zone are written only to that zone's shards, queries that include `country` are targeted, and queries that omit it are broadcast to every shard. Zones should be defined before a collection is sharded, because changing them later triggers large migrations ([MongoDB](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/manual/tutorial/sharding-segmenting-data-by-location/)). For Nabd, this means the market key should be the ISO code itself (`countryCode: 'SA'`), and it should be the **first field of the main compound indexes from day one**. That keeps a later move to zone sharding, or splitting a country out into its own cell, cheap. Everything else about a country (currency, tax, regulators, enabled services) belongs in a separate `countries` document.

### Deployment: one template, N cells, a thin global directory

The deciding question is whether one deployment can serve every country. Data law answers it for health. **UAE Federal Law 2/2019 Art. 13 forbids, by default, storing or processing outside the UAE any health data about health services provided in the UAE.** Ministerial Resolution 51/2021 lists ten exceptions, granted case by case by the emirate health authority ([Baker McKenzie](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/data-technology/united-arab-emirates-health-data-law-permitted-transfers-of-health-data)), and Abu Dhabi's DoH applies strict localisation ([Baker McKenzie](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/data-technology/uae-abu-dhabi-department-of-health-requires-licensed-entities-to-comply-with-strict-data-localization-requirements)). **Egypt's PDPL executive regulations require a PDPC licence for any transfer abroad, including cloud hosting, with a 90-working-day review and compliance expected from about 1 Nov 2026** [uncertain start date] ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/are/legal-updates/egypt-s-pdpl-executive-regulations-issued-one-year-compliance-countdown-begins)). **Saudi Arabia's SDAIA draft amendments (consultation 6 Oct to 5 Nov 2026) include an Art. 23 that would require storage in the Kingdom** [pending] ([Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/10/sdaia-publishes-draft-amendments-to-the-pdpl)). The NCA Cloud Cybersecurity Controls already carry localisation controls ([NCA](https://nca.gov.sa/en/regulatory-documents/controls-list/ccc/)). The NHIC telehealth guideline says platforms should be hosted in Saudi Arabia ([NHIC](https://nhic.gov.sa/standards/Telehealth/Telehealth-Application-Guidelines.pdf)). Dubai's Telehealth Standard v4 (in effect 26 Nov 2025) requires UAE-hosted servers and NABIDH integration ([Sigosoft, secondary](https://sigosoft.com/blog/dha-telehealth-standards-checklist/)).

The recommended topology is therefore **one codebase, one deployment template, and one cell per residency jurisdiction**: the KSA cell in-Kingdom first, which is R19's existing plan, and a UAE cell when the UAE launches. Above the cells sits a **thin global layer that holds no health data**: an account directory (`hash(phone) → homeCountry, cell`), the country configuration, and the marketing site shell. Countries without a confirmed localisation rule (Bahrain, Kuwait, Jordan) can share a cell only where counsel confirms the transfer route. For example, Bahrain allows transfers to "white-list" countries and needs PDPA approval otherwise, and breaching the transfer rules is a criminal offence ([Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2022/04/bahrain-issues-new-privacy-guidelines)). Whether Saudi Arabia is on Bahrain's white-list was not found. This also explains why R19 matters beyond Saudi Arabia: the current production host is in Europe, and R19 lists Cloudflare R2, Sentry (EU), e-mail providers, public Nominatim and AI providers as processors outside the Kingdom. Each cell needs that same inventory, and `residency-inventory.spec.ts` should become per-cell.

### Country detection, travellers and URLs

Google's guidance is direct: **do not adapt content or auto-redirect by IP or Accept-Language. Show a visible country and language switcher on every page and let the URL carry the market** ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites)). Nabd should resolve the market in this order: the URL on the web or a stored choice in the app, then the account's `homeCountry`, then the E.164 prefix of the sign-up phone, then the SIM country, then IP, then device locale, falling back to `SA`. Detection only pre-selects a market and offers a dismissible "switch?" banner. Three fields stay separate: **`homeCountry`** (where the account and its health records live), **`currentMarket`** (where the user is browsing or booking) and **`uiLanguage`**. A Saudi patient in Dubai keeps a KSA account. Booking a Dubai doctor becomes a cross-border case, and the rules below govern it.

For URLs, Google lists ccTLDs, subdomains and subdirectories as valid and discourages parameters. Subdirectories on one host are the low-maintenance option ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites)), and noon uses country-language segments such as `/saudi-en/` ([noon Partners](https://helpcenter.noon.partners/en/category/program-policies/ksa-seller-terms-and-conditions-tandcs)). Nabd's natural form is `/{cc}-{lang}/` (`/sa-ar/`, `/ae-en/`). hreflang needs ISO 639-1 language codes with an optional region, bidirectional links, and an `x-default` pointing to a country selector ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/localized-versions)). One concrete snag for Nabd's six languages: **`fil` is not an ISO 639-1 code, so the Filipino pages probably need `tl` in hreflang** [unverified whether Google accepts `fil`]. Each country-language page should canonicalise to itself, never across countries, because prices, currency and providers differ. Doctor and pharmacy profiles belong to one market. MULTI-COUNTRY.md already warns that adding a country dimension changes canonical URLs, sitemaps and the nginx bot rule (`WEB-BOT-LIMIT.md`). The safe sequence is to **leave today's Saudi URLs untouched until a second country is close to launch**, then move them under `/sa-{lang}/` in one step with 301 redirects, per-market sitemaps and hreflang. This is a judgement call: keeping Saudi Arabia at the root is also defensible, but it makes Saudi Arabia a special case forever.

### Catalogue, pricing and feature flags

Shopify's model fits: a market's **catalog** restricts what it sells (excluded items are hidden from search and blocked from the cart), and prices come from automatic conversion or, preferably, **fixed local price lists**. Shopify tells apps to load each market's prices rather than derive them from a base price ([Shopify.dev](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/markets)). Nabd needs a **global definition plus a per-country offering**. Global records are specialties, service definitions, lab tests, radiology studies and drug ingredients, with translations and codes. Each per-country offering is a `MarketOffering { countryCode, refId, enabled, price{amount_minor,currency}, taxCode, regulatoryCodes }`. Drugs are the exception: registration is national (SFDA, Egypt's EDA with its mandatory unified coding under Decree 161/2025 ([ADSERO](https://adsero.me/the-egyptian-drug-authority-issues-decree-to-standardise-pharmaceuticals-and-medical-supplies-coding-and-traceability/)), and the UAE's drug authority [unverified]). The pharmacy catalogue should therefore be **per-country reference data** linked by an ingredient code. MULTI-COUNTRY.md makes the same point: a global catalogue with Saudi registration numbers would show wrong data abroad. Feature flags take `country` as a targeting attribute, so video, pharmacy, lab, radiology, home care and insurance switch on per market. Clients fetch `/countries/{cc}/config` at start.

## The data model: country on every record, money as minor units, plugins resolved by country

### The `countries` document

MULTI-COUNTRY.md already proposes a `countries` collection read through one cached resolver, like `finance_config`. The research adds fields and makes them **effective-dated**. VAT rates have moved before (KSA 5% to 15% in 2020, Bahrain to 10% in 2022), and e-invoicing mandates arrive on fixed dates (UAE 2027, Oman 2027), so these changes must be data, not deploys ([ZATCA](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Guideline-For-the-Healthcare-Sector-under-VAT-Provisions.pdf); [AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/analysis/economy/2023/06/differing-vat-rates-complicate-intra-gcc-trade/)). A minimal row holds:

| Group | Fields |
|---|---|
| Identity | `code` (ISO alpha-2), `jurisdictions[]` (ISO 3166-2 where health regulation is sub-national, e.g. `AE-DU`), `enabled`, `launchStage` (`internal` / `dark` / `open`), `cell` |
| Money | `currency`, `minor_unit`, `settlementCurrency`, `cashRounding` (unknown for 3-decimal cash, ask the accountant), `paymentGateways[]`, `paymentMethods[]` |
| Tax | `taxRules[] {taxCode, rate, validFrom, validTo, recipientType, nationalityRule}`, `invoiceProvider {type, b2b, b2c, validFrom}`, `invoiceRounding` |
| Locale | `defaultLocale`, `locales[]`, `numberingSystem`, `timezone`, `weekendDays`, `holidayCalendarId` |
| Contact | `phone {callingCode, defaultRegion}`, `sms {provider, senderId, templates{locale→id}}`, `otpChannels[]`, `supportPhone` |
| Identity docs | `idTypes[] {type, validator, requiredFor[]}`, `identityProviders[]` |
| Address | `addressSchema`, `nationalAddressType`, `geocoder` |

| Legal and privacy | `legalDocs {type→locale→version}`, `privacy {regulator, breachHours, dpoRequired, permits[]}`, `refundPolicy` |

### `country_code` on records, and who owns the country

Every market-scoped collection gets `countryCode`: users (as `homeCountry`), provider profiles and accounts, facilities, pharmacies, services and prices, appointments, pharmacy orders and offers, lab and radiology orders, home-care bookings, transactions, payouts, ledger entries, invoices, promotions and content. MULTI-COUNTRY.md states the ownership rule: **a booking's country is the provider's, and a refund's country is the payment's**. A request-scoped `MarketContext` (a NestJS interceptor with CLS, resolved from `X-Market`, the URL or the account) plus a **repository guard that rejects queries on market-scoped collections without `countryCode`** turns cross-market leaks into errors, the way tenant filters do. Every main compound index starts with `countryCode`, which is the MongoDB zone-sharding precondition ([MongoDB](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/manual/tutorial/sharding-segmenting-data-by-location/)).

### Money: R11 is already step one

The research and the code agree. **Store integer minor units plus an ISO 4217 code, take the exponent from a table, never hard-code `/100`, never add amounts in different currencies, and allocate remainders explicitly** so the parts of a split sum to the whole ([Fowler](https://martinfowler.com/eaaCatalog/money.html); [Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/currencies)). R11's `*_halalas` shadows are exactly `{amount_minor}` for SAR. MULTI-COUNTRY.md's ordering is right: **finish R11 P3 (stop writing the doubles) first, then rename the integer shadow into `{ amount_minor, currency }`**, so there is one migration and not two. Two traps are specific to the region. First, a 3-decimal currency pushed through a 2-decimal path loses a factor of ten. One forum report describes an OMR order of 599.000 arriving as 59.900 ([WordPress forum, anecdotal](https://wordpress.org/support/users/motasemabudahab/replies/)). Second, some PSPs reject 3-decimal amounts whose last digit is not 0 ([Checkout.com](https://www.checkout.com/docs/payments/accept-payments/format-the-amount-value.md)). Each gateway plugin therefore owns its own amount codec.

### Multi-currency ledger rules

`finance-integrity/ledger-invariants.ts` assumes one currency today. The rules to adopt are these. Ledger accounts are keyed per `(owner, currency)`. Every invariant (debits equal credits, wallet equals sum of entries) is evaluated **per currency**. An order is priced and collected in the provider's market currency through that market's PSP entity. Refunds go back in the original presentment currency, which Shopify treats as the source of truth for refunds ([Shopify.dev](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/markets)). Commission and payout splits use remainder allocation. Any FX is an explicit pair of entries with a stored rate. Cross-country reporting converts with a rate snapshot and never mutates ledger amounts. Each country is expected to have its own legal entity and merchant account settling in local currency ([Amazon Payment Services](https://paymentservices.amazon.com/docs/payment-methods/payment-methods-support)), so the ledger is effectively **one book per entity**, and a wallet balance never crosses entities.

### Per-country strategy plugins

The payment seam in `payments/payment-gateway.ts` is the pattern to copy. It changes from `PAYMENT_PROVIDER` read once per deployment to `gateway(countryCode)` resolved per request, with one or more gateways per country. The same shape applies to the plugins below. The owner decision on licences fits the research well: **the notes found no public verification API for SCFHS or any other regulator, so "provider uploads the licence, an admin verifies it, a periodic re-check" is the generic default for every jurisdiction**, not a Saudi workaround. `common/scfhs-license.ts` and the `requiresScfhs` checks become `licenceRegistry(jurisdiction)` with per-jurisdiction document types and expiry rules. `SCFHS_VERIFY_URL` and the SCFHS field names can then fold into a generic `licences[] {jurisdiction, regulator, number, documentUrl, verifiedBy, verifiedAt, expiresAt}`. One correction to carry forward: in the UAE the jurisdiction is the emirate, so a Dubai (DHA) licence does not cover an Abu Dhabi patient ([Kayrouz](https://www.kayrouzandassociates.com/insights/licensing-and-data-rules-for-telehealth-providers-in-the-uae)).

### Cross-border: deny by default

Physical services (pharmacy delivery, lab and radiology visits, home nursing) should be **strictly same-market**: order country = provider country = delivery-address country. For video, the evidence points to the patient's jurisdiction. Qatar excludes care delivered from outside Qatar ([DHP](https://dhp.moph.gov.qa/en/QCHPCirculars/Eng-Circular.pdf)). Dubai allows a doctor abroad only if DHA-licensed and affiliated to a DHA facility, and limits care for residents travelling abroad to advice and follow-up ([UAE Expert Hub, secondary](https://www.uaeexperthub.com/telemedicine-uae-rules-what-is-allowed/)). A `crossBorderPolicy {patientCountry, providerCountry, service, allowed, reason}` table defaulting to deny is the right shape. Matching should require "a practitioner licensed in the patient's jurisdiction plus a local facility of record". Payments and data rules point the same way: Stripe's self-serve cross-border payouts are limited by region ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/cross-border-payouts)), and moving health data out of the UAE or Egypt needs an exception or a licence.

### Fitting the owner's existing client decisions

Two existing decisions need a market dimension. The **local-first cart** (pharmacy orders are broadcast and answered with offers, and the cart lives on the device) must store `countryCode` and `currency` with the cart. Switching markets then starts a separate cart instead of mixing SAR and AED lines, and sending the order still needs the backend. The **six-language translation rule** collides with Saudi SMS: because CST requires every SMS to match a registered template ([Infobip](https://www.infobip.com/docs/essentials/mena-registration/saudi-arabia-letter-of-authorization-loa-guidelines/local-sms-sender-and-template-registration-guideline)), OTP text cannot be free-form. Each language's OTP template must be registered per country, or OTP SMS stays Arabic and English only with in-app text in the user's language.

Each phase can ship on its own and, until Phase 4, changes nothing a Saudi user can see. The gate column turns "no behaviour change" into tests: every existing amount and invoice must be byte-for-byte the same, as MULTI-COUNTRY.md requires.

### Which second country

| Criterion | UAE (Dubai first) | Bahrain | Egypt | Kuwait |

| Engineering cost | highest (second cell) | lowest | high (payments + licence) | medium, legal uncertainty |

**Recommendation: build Phases 0-4 so they are UAE-capable, and choose the UAE, launching in Dubai only, as the second country if the owner can fund a UAE entity, a DHA-licensed facility arrangement and a UAE cell.** It is the strongest business case, it reuses the most payment and BNPL integrations, its B2C e-invoicing is exempt for now, and it forces the cell architecture that every later health market will need anyway. **If budget or hosting cannot stretch to a second cell in 2027, Bahrain is the better second country**: one regulator, an existing telemedicine framework, a 3-decimal currency that proves the money model, and possibly no second cell, provided counsel confirms the PDPA transfer route. Egypt should wait. It brings a new payment stack, a transfer licence with a 90-working-day review, no telemedicine or e-pharmacy law, and an established local competitor. Kuwait's lack of any telemedicine framework makes it a legal question before it is a product one. This recommendation weighs evidence from the notes against judgement. Market-size, demand and competitor data were not researched, and they should be gathered before the owner decides.

### What counsel and the accountant must confirm first

The open items that block code or launch are these. For **Saudi Arabia**: whether in-Kingdom hosting is mandatory for Nabd's licence (NHIC, NCA, and the PDPL draft Art. 23 [pending]); whether Nabd needs its own MOH telehealth licence or works through licensed facilities (sources conflict ([Neelim, secondary](https://www.neelim.com/blog/telemedicine-licensing-gcc-2026))); whether Wasfaty integration is mandatory for platform prescriptions; SFDA e-pharmacy and delivery licensing (no source found); the agent-versus-principal VAT treatment of the marketplace and who issues the ZATCA invoice; the Royal Order A/86 citizen mechanism and 0% medicines on Nabd's invoices; and the VAT and e-invoicing details that INVOICE-REVIEW.md already lists as unsettled. For **any second country**: practitioner and facility licensing, including home nursing, lab and radiology licensing, for which no sources were found in any country; residency, permits and transfer routes; controlled-drug and sick-leave limits; recording and consent rules for video, which were not confirmed anywhere; consumer-law carve-outs for dispensed medicines and delivered consultations; digital consent age and age of majority, which were not sourced for any country; cash rounding for 3-decimal currencies; and VAT registration thresholds and deemed-supplier rules [unverified]. Phone number lengths, national ID formats beyond KSA and Egypt, weekend days and CLDR digit defaults are marked as background knowledge in the notes and should be checked against libphonenumber and CLDR before the validators ship.

## Conclusion

Nabd does not lack a multi-country design. Its own inventory has one, and this research mostly confirms its shape. The research does change the ordering of risk. Hard-coded `'SAR'` is a few weeks of mechanical work. The real constraints sit in health law: licences stop at borders and emirate lines, health data stays home by default, and no regulator offers a verification API. Two consequences follow. First, the owner's "upload and admin-verify" licence decision and R19's in-Kingdom move are not Saudi-only chores. They are the generic machinery every future country will reuse, so they should be built as per-jurisdiction plugins and as a cell template, not as one-off fixes. Second, the expensive part of country number two is legal entities, licences and a hosting cell, not code. The engineering goal for 2027 should therefore be to make a new country cost as little code as possible, mostly a config row, a few plugin configs and a cell, so that the owner's choice of where to go next is a business and legal decision rather than an engineering one.
